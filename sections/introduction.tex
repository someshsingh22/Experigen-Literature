\section {Our contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item We observed the agentic hivemind problem, which talks about how LLMs are not good at generating diverse and feasible hypotheses for social sciences in both inter and intra model setups. No. of generations doesn't affect this. It will only generate 2 semantically diverse personas in a batch of 10 otherwise it'll use synonyms at best.

\item In this paper we introduce a social agent framework. In this framework we use X datasets and X personas that are representative of their respective datasets. We've used demographic prompting to understand if age,gender,nation helps in diversifying. We benchmarked these against human literature by retrieving papers from the internet. We used metrics like vendi score and construct diversity to understand actual no. of distinct mechanisms in a batch.

\item We also introduce a novel algorithm to synthetically generate such agent populations, our method consistently outperform all previous fixed set of personas by X across X tasks. We show that our algorithm gives consistent gains across sizes and attributes of populations.
\end{enumerate} 
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Persona Scaling — Open RQs, TODOs \& Ideas}

\paragraph{Research Questions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Persona Count):} How does the number of personas affect the diversity \emph{and} accuracy of generated hypotheses? What is the optimal number of personas?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Persona Detail):} How detailed should personas be for this task? Which attributes are most important? What is the best $(\text{size} \times \text{attributes})$ configuration?
    \emph{(RQ2 is harder and downstream of RQ1.)}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Current Finding}
Personas improve hypothesis diversity; more target-population-informed personas yield better diversity. However, diversity $\neq$ accuracy — random personas would be diverse but inaccurate. Dataset knowledge is essential for accuracy.
